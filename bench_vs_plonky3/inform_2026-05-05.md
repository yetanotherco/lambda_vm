> **Nota (2026-05-13):** los números de este reporte fueron tomados con el

## TL;DR

- **L/P3 prove time gap is 1.78× / 1.76× / 1.93×** at log-rows 17 / 19 / 21
  (default 32 columns, blowup=2). Reproducible to ±1% across sessions.
- **At log=19 constraint eval is 51% of the gap** (Lambda 3.5× slower at this
  phase). At log=21 the gap distributes: constraint eval 32%, trace LDE 26%,
  FRI commit 16%. The shape of the problem changes with N.
- **Lambda scales worse than P3 with blowup.** Doubling blowup widens the L/P3
  gap by +13% per doubling. Going from blowup=2 to blowup=4 at log=21 costs
  Lambda +1240 ms.
- **The L/P3 gap degrades linearly with column count.** From 8 to 128 columns
  the ratio goes 1.57× → 2.88×. Lambda VM real (70-200 cols) is in the
  2.5-3.5× zone, not the 1.93× of the default bench.
- **Decision: pivot to FRI plan** (`PLAN-fri-optimizations.md`: folding=4 +
  early-stop). Quotient-domain optimisation that requires raising blowup is
  not viable for Lambda — penalty +1240 ms exceeds the constraint-eval
  ceiling 482 ms by ~1.6×.

## Setup

- **Server**: `vm-benchmarks-1`, AMD EPYC 9454P (48 cores, Zen 4, 12-channel
  DDR5).
- **Arch**: x86_64.
- **SIMD**: scalar mode on both — `RUSTFLAGS="-C
  target-feature=-avx2,-avx512f"`. Residual SSE2 in p3-keccak (~7%) is the
  only remaining vector path.
- **AIR**: shared Fibonacci, cell-by-cell equivalent between Lambda and P3.
  Default 32 columns (`num-sequences=16`), blowup=2, 219 FRI queries,
  grinding=0, degree-3 extension on both sides (`p3-goldilocks-patched`
  fork).
- **Measurement**: single-shot end-to-end prove. **10 runs per (config),
  median + CV reported.** Default rayon (no thread pinning).
- **Branch**: `bench_vs_p3_improvements`. Harness improvement (`--blowup`
  flag added to `run.sh`) was applied directly on the server during this
  session; not committed yet.

| Phase | log=19 ratio | log=21 ratio | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Constraint eval | 3.53× | 2.72× | **improves** |
| Trace LDE+Merkle | 1.49× | 1.87× | **worsens** |

phases.

## Bench 3 — Blowup sweep

The decisive bench. Question: when blowup goes from 2 to 4 (or 8), does
Lambda scale better, equal, or worse than P3? This determines whether the
quotient-domain optimisation (which on degree-3 AIRs requires raising blowup
to remain mathematically faithful) is viable.

| log | blowup | L prove | P3 prove | **L/P3** | L proof | L peak RSS |
|-----|-------:|--------:|---------:|---------:|--------:|-----------:|
| 19  | 2 | 0.570 s | 0.318 s | **1.79×** | 4.12 MB | 0.82 GB |
| 19  | 4 | 0.921 s | 0.471 s | **1.95×** | 4.42 MB | 1.50 GB |
| 19  | 8 | 1.613 s | 0.738 s | **2.19×** | 4.73 MB | 2.84 GB |
| 21  | 2 | 1.897 s | 0.972 s | **1.95×** | 4.80 MB | 3.30 GB |
| 21  | 4 | 3.137 s | 1.443 s | **2.17×** | 5.13 MB | 5.99 GB |
| 21  | 8 | 5.760 s | 2.334 s | **2.47×** | 5.46 MB | 11.4 GB |

CVs across all 6 runs: 0.46%-7.04%. Tight.

### How each prover scales with blowup

At log=21, doubling blowup multiplies prove time by:

| blowup step | Lambda factor | P3 factor |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2 → 4 | 1.65× | 1.49× |
| 4 → 8 | 1.83× | 1.62× |

P3's blowup penalty is sub-linear (more efficient). Lambda's is closer to
linear and getting worse at higher blowup. **Each doubling of blowup widens
the L/P3 gap by ~+13%.**

### Decision math

If Lambda implemented quotient-domain at blowup=4 (the minimum needed for
degree-3 AIRs to work mathematically with that approach):

- **Saving from quotient-domain at log=21**: ≈ Lambda's `r2_constraints`
  budget = **482 ms** (assumes the optimisation eliminates this entirely;
  upper bound).
- **Penalty from blowup 2→4 at log=21**: 3.137 − 1.897 = **+1240 ms**.
- **Net at log=21**: −482 + 1240 = **+758 ms slower**.

Same calculation at log=19:

- Saving: ≤ 171.5 ms (constraint eval).
- Penalty: 0.921 − 0.570 = +351 ms.
- Net: +180 ms slower.

**The optimisation costs more than it saves at every measured size.**

## Bench 4 — Column sweep at log=21

| cols | n  | L prove | P3 prove | L/P3 |
|-----:|---:|--------:|---------:|-----:|
| 8    | 4  | 1.297 s | 0.829 s | **1.57×** |
| 16   | 8  | 1.459 s | 0.881 s | 1.66× |
| 32   | 16 | 1.886 s | 0.975 s | 1.93× (default bench config) |
| 64   | 32 | 2.742 s | 1.172 s | 2.34× |
| 128  | 64 | 4.723 s | 1.643 s | **2.88×** |

Linear degradation with column count. Lambda's per-column FFT structure
means each extra column adds proportionally; P3's batched FFT amortises.
**Going from 8 to 128 columns moves L/P3 from 1.57× to 2.88× — a 1.83×
widening.**

### Production translation

Lambda VM real has tables with 70-200 columns (Keccak, CPU, Bitwise, AUX).
Linearly extrapolating the sweep:

| #cols | predicted L/P3 (log=21) |
|------:|------------------------:|
| 70    | ~2.4× |
| 100   | ~2.6× |
| 150   | ~2.8× |
| 200   | ~3.1× |

**The default bench (32 cols) under-predicts the production gap by ~1.5×.**
The real ratio Lambda VM users see in production is closer to 2.5-3×.

## Decision

### Pivot to FRI plan (`PLAN-fri-optimizations.md`)

Why:
- **Blowup-independent.** Does not enter the regime where Lambda scales
  worse with blowup.
- **Attacks FRI commit**, the third-largest gap component (15.8% of gap at
  log=21, 14.1% at log=19) — meaningful.
- **Reduces proof size 2.0× → ~1.2×** vs P3 — independent bandwidth win that
  matters even if latency improvement is modest.
- **Universal**: helps any AIR (deg-1, deg-3, more), any blowup, any size.
- ~1 week of focused work; plan already detailed step-by-step.

### Quotient-domain (raise-blowup variant): discarded

The constraint-eval bottleneck is real (50.7% of gap at log=19) but the only
mechanism to attack it under the current evaluator (subir blowup) costs more
than it saves on Lambda. A direct port of P3's approach is therefore not the
move.

### Open: quotient-domain WITHOUT raising blowup

A third path is unexplored: **a quotient-domain optimisation that operates on
the existing 2N LDE without bumping blowup**. The mathematical question is
whether the degree-3 composition polynomial can be commited from the 2N
evaluations without loss of soundness. Lambda's existing
`interpolate + break_in_parts` fallback already does *something* like this
(commits to a projection P of degree < 2N rather than the full H). Whether
P3's quotient-domain idea can be adapted to Lambda's projection scheme is
unknown.

This is the natural next research question if FRI lands and the gap is still
too large for production. Out of scope for now.

## Throughput note (carried over from prior parallelism sweep)

From the older `inform.md` sweeps (T=1..96 at log=21): Lambda's parallelism
ceiling is ~8.25× (Amdahl serial fraction ~10.6%); P3 reaches 16.4× at
T=40. In throughput mode (multiple proofs concurrent at T=24 each) Lambda
can reach an effective ~2.1 s per proof on this server — better than its
single-proof T=48 of 3.6 s. **Worth validating against batch workloads
post-FRI.**
